CLIMATE FACTORS KNOWLEDGE BASE

[CLIM-01] Rainfall Variability and Species Survival
In low-rainfall / semi-arid regions, water availability is typically the primary limiting factor for both crop yield and native biodiversity. Species richness of ground-cover vegetation and associated insect life correlates strongly with soil moisture retention, meaning interventions that improve infiltration and reduce evaporation (mulching, agroforestry shade, contour bunding) have outsized biodiversity benefits in dry regions compared to high-rainfall regions.
Metric impacted: Soil moisture, species survival rate.
Time horizon: Short-term (within 1 season for mulching); medium-term for shade-tree effects.
Source: IPCC AR6 Working Group II, chapter on water scarcity and ecosystems.

[CLIM-02] Temperature Stress and Microbial Activity
Soil microbial activity (key to nutrient cycling and organic matter breakdown) declines sharply above 35°C soil surface temperature. Shade cover from intercropping or agroforestry can reduce peak soil surface temperatures by 5-8°C, keeping microbial activity within optimal ranges for longer daily periods.
Metric impacted: Microbial activity, nutrient cycling rate.
Time horizon: Immediate (same season) once shade cover is established.
Source: FAO Soil Biodiversity report; IPCC AR6 agricultural systems chapter.

[CLIM-03] Water Harvesting and Contour Techniques
In low-rainfall areas, contour bunding, swales, and small-scale water harvesting structures can increase effective soil moisture by capturing and slowing runoff. This raises effective water availability for both crops and native vegetation without requiring additional irrigation infrastructure, with documented yield and vegetation cover improvements of 20-30% in semi-arid pilot programs.
Metric impacted: Soil moisture, vegetation cover, species survival.
Time horizon: Short-term (first rainy season after construction).
Source: FAO Water harvesting techniques for dryland agriculture.

[CLIM-04] Climate-Biodiversity Feedback Loop
Reduced vegetation cover (from monoculture or degraded land) lowers local evapotranspiration, which can reduce local humidity and rainfall recycling in already dry regions—a feedback that further stresses biodiversity. Restoring vegetation cover (agroforestry, cover crops) helps stabilize local microclimate over time.
Metric impacted: Local microclimate stability, vegetation cover.
Time horizon: Long-term (multi-year cumulative effect).
Source: IPCC AR6 WG1 land-atmosphere interactions chapter.

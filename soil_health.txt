SOIL HEALTH KNOWLEDGE BASE

[SOIL-01] Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) and Biodiversity Link
Soil organic carbon below 0.5% is generally classified as degraded/low-fertility soil, common in semi-arid monoculture systems. SOC above 1.5-2% supports significantly higher soil microbial diversity, which underpins nutrient cycling and plant health. Low SOC soils show reduced microbial biomass, fewer earthworm populations, and lower water infiltration capacity.
Source: FAO Global Soil Partnership guidelines on soil organic carbon sequestration (2017-2020 series).

[SOIL-02] Cover Cropping and Legume Intercropping
Introducing legume-based cover crops (e.g., clover, cowpea, vetch) between main crop cycles fixes atmospheric nitrogen via rhizobia symbiosis and adds organic matter to soil. Field trials summarized by FAO indicate SOC increases of approximately 15-25% over 2-3 years under consistent cover cropping, alongside measurable increases in microbial diversity and pollinator visitation due to flowering cover species.
Metric impacted: Soil organic carbon, microbial diversity, pollinator abundance.
Time horizon: Medium-term (2-3 years for measurable SOC gains).
Source: FAO Conservation Agriculture program studies; IPCC AR6 WG3 agricultural mitigation chapter.

[SOIL-03] Agroforestry and Soil Carbon in Semi-Arid Systems
In semi-arid regions with low rainfall, converting monoculture cropland to agroforestry (integrating trees like Acacia, Moringa, or Faidherbia albida with crops) improves soil structure, increases shade-driven moisture retention, and can raise SOC by 20-40% over 5-10 years compared to monoculture baselines. Tree root systems also reduce topsoil erosion by up to 50% in sloped or wind-exposed terrain.
Metric impacted: Soil organic carbon, soil moisture retention, erosion control.
Time horizon: Medium to long-term (5-10 years for full canopy/root benefit).
Source: World Agroforestry Centre (ICRAF) semi-arid systems research; FAO Agroforestry for Land Restoration reports.

[SOIL-04] Soil pH and Nutrient Availability
Soil pH outside the 6.0-7.5 range restricts nutrient availability (notably phosphorus and micronutrients), which indirectly suppresses plant diversity by favoring only pH-tolerant species. Amendments like lime (for acidic soils) or gypsum (for alkaline/sodic soils) can restore pH balance within 1-2 growing seasons.
Metric impacted: Nutrient availability, plant species diversity.
Time horizon: Short-term (1-2 seasons).
Source: FAO Soils Portal nutrient management guidelines.

[SOIL-05] No-Till and Reduced Tillage Practices
Reducing or eliminating tillage preserves soil structure, fungal networks (mycorrhizae), and carbon stores that plowing disrupts. No-till systems show 10-20% higher SOC retention over 3-5 years versus conventional tillage, and support greater soil arthropod diversity.
Metric impacted: Soil organic carbon, soil biodiversity (arthropods, fungi).
Time horizon: Medium-term (3-5 years).
Source: FAO Conservation Agriculture technical guidelines.
